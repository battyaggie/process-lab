Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. This process is a hydrogen fuel-cell car: how the hydrogen from the tank and the air make electricity in the fuel cell stack, how the electricity is converted for the electric motor and the battery, how the water leaves through the exhaust and how the stack is cooled. The 3D model shows a generic transparent car at real size in six stations. Part 1 uses 11 numbered parts.

At a glance

Times are suggestions. Adjust them to your group and level.

Before class

  1. Part 1 is an online drag-and-drop exercise: nothing needs to be printed. Students check their own answers on screen.
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs. They drag each name from the word box to the right number: a correct name snaps into place, a wrong one jumps back. The station pictures below the table show the steps in more detail.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is stored, is reduced, is supplied, is converted, is released, is cooled.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 11 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: hydrogen is white bubbles in a pale blue tube, air is light blue, water vapor is white, direct current (DC) is red, alternating current (AC) is yellow, the coolant is dark blue and the heat is an orange glow. In reality hydrogen is invisible; the color is added. No flame is shown.
  • Students often confuse the DC/DC converter (part 6, adjusts the voltage) with the inverter (part 8, DC to AC), the battery (part 7) with the fuel cell stack (part 4), the air compressor (part 3) with the coolant pump (part 10), and the radiator (part 11, gives the heat to the air) with the exhaust (part 5, releases the water vapor). The stack (part 4) appears in stations 2, 3, 4 and 6.
  • The stations follow the energy and the materials: 1 hydrogen supply (the pressure number falls from 700 bar), 2 air supply, 3 reaction in the stack (an enlarged cell with the anode, the membrane and the cathode; the loop repeats; it is the reverse of the electrolyzer exercise), 4 water exhaust, 5 power to the wheels (the end shows braking: the dots run backwards because the motor works as a generator), 6 cooling. The car is a generic transparent shell at real size, about 5 m long.
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from Wasserstofftechnik to look at it before the first class.
  • Other exercises are on the same site. Each one has its own address.
