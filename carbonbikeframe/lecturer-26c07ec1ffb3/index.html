Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. A carbon-fiber frame is very light and very strong. The material is called prepreg: carbon fibers that are already soaked in epoxy resin. Because the resin starts to harden at room temperature, the prepreg is stored in the freezer. At the start of production, the ply cutter takes a roll of prepreg and cuts it automatically into plies, which are pieces with exact shapes. Workers lay the plies at the layup table, layer by layer, into the two halves of the mold. The direction of the fibers in each layer is important, because the fibers give the stiffness in their direction. Inside the tubes, the workers insert a bladder, a thin inflatable tube. Then they close the mold. The closed mold goes into the heated press. The press heats the mold to about 130 degrees Celsius, and the air compressor inflates the bladder. The bladder pushes the plies against the wall of the mold, and the resin hardens under heat and pressure. This takes about an hour. After cooling, the workers open the mold, take out the frame and pull out the bladder. The trimming machine cuts off the excess material at the edges. The ultrasonic scanner then checks the frame for hidden air pockets. Finally, the frame goes to the paint booth. The 3D model has seven stations. Part 1 uses 10 numbered parts.

Before class

  1. Part 1 is an online drag-and-drop exercise: nothing needs to be printed. Students check their own answers on screen.
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs. They drag each name from the word box to the right number: a correct name snaps into place, a wrong one jumps back. The station pictures below the table show the steps in more detail.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is stored, is cut, are laid, is inflated, is cured, is trimmed, is checked, is painted.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 10 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: prepreg and frame are black, cold air is light blue, compressed air is shown as white dots, the ultrasound waves are purple, the scan result is green (good) or red (defect), the paint is red. In the press the plies glow from blue to orange as they heat up.
  • A real cure takes about an hour at about 130 degrees Celsius; the clips are slow motion. The workers are not shown: the hoist and the carrier move the mold and the frame by themselves.
  • Students often confuse the mold (the steel tool) and the bladder (the inflatable tube inside the frame tubes), the heated press (closes and heats) and the air compressor (supplies air), and the trimming machine (cuts the edges) and the ultrasonic scanner (looks inside the material). Parts 4 and 5 appear in several stations.
  • The stations: 1 cold storage and cutting (rolls leave the freezer, the cutter cuts plies), 2 layup (plies are laid in the mold; inset shows the fiber angles), 3 bladder and closing the mold (bladders slide into the tubes, the upper half closes), 4 curing (the mold goes into the press, the plates heat up, the compressor inflates the bladder, the temperature rises), 5 demolding and trimming (the press opens, the frame is lifted out, the trimming machine cuts off the excess), 6 inspection (the scanner probe moves along the tube, a red spot shows an air pocket), 7 painting (the frame is turned upright and the spray gun paints it red).
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from Maschinenbau or Sports Engineering to look at it before the first class.
