Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. Injection molding produces plastic parts, such as the housing of a power tool, in large numbers. Plastic granules are stored in the hopper above the machine. They fall into the barrel, where a rotating screw moves them forward. The heater bands around the barrel melt the plastic at about 220 degrees Celsius, and the friction of the screw adds more heat. While the screw collects the melted plastic in front of it, the clamping unit closes the two halves of the mold and holds them together with great force. Then the screw moves forward like a piston and injects the melt through the nozzle into the mold. For a short time, the machine keeps the pressure high to fill the mold completely. The cooling channels in the mold carry water, which cools the plastic until it is solid. After that, the clamping unit opens the mold, and the ejector pins push the finished part out. The take-out robot takes the part and puts it on the conveyor. Then the clamping unit closes again and the next cycle starts. The 3D model has six stations. Part 1 uses 11 numbered parts.

Before class

  1. Print or share the student vocabulary sheet (or let students use it on screen from the Part 1 link).
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs on the sheet. The names are not given in the pictures, so they have to use the word box and the station pictures.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is stored, is melted, is injected, is cooled, is pushed out, is taken out.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 11 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: granules are white, melt is orange, cooling water is shown as blue dots, the finished part turns from orange to blue-gray.
  • A real cycle is about 20 to 60 seconds; the clips are slow motion. The heating temperature in the text is about 220 degrees Celsius.
  • Students often confuse the barrel (heated tube), the screw (turns inside the barrel and later pushes like a piston) and the nozzle (narrow opening into the mold), the clamping unit (the machine that closes the mold) and the mold (the tool with the shape of the part), and the ejector pins (inside the mold) and the take-out robot (outside, grips the part). Parts 3, 6 and 7 appear in several stations.
  • The stations: 1 feeding (granules fall from the hopper into the barrel, the screw turns), 2 melting (heater bands glow, the granules turn into orange melt), 3 closing the mold (the clamping unit moves, the halves close), 4 injection (the screw moves forward, the melt fills the cavity through the nozzle), 5 cooling (water flows in the cooling channels, the part turns blue-gray), 6 ejection (the mold opens, the ejector pins push, the robot puts the part on the conveyor).
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from Maschinenbau or Wirtschaftsingenieurwesen to look at it before the first class.
