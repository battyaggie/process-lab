Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. This process is the impact test of a bicycle helmet according to the standard EN 1078: the helmet is stored in the climate chamber (cold, hot or wet), placed on the headform with the accelerometer, lifted along the guide wires of the drop rig to 1.5 meters and released, falls onto the anvil at about 5.4 meters per second while the high-speed camera films the impact, and the data acquisition system and the evaluation software record and evaluate the acceleration. The peak must stay below 250 g, otherwise the helmet fails.

Before class

  1. Part 1 is an online drag-and-drop exercise: nothing needs to be printed. Students check their own answers on screen.
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs. They drag each name from the word box to the right number: a correct name snaps into place, a wrong one jumps back. The station pictures below the table show the steps in more detail.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is stored, is placed, is lifted, is released, falls, hits, is crushed, is recorded, is evaluated. Also result clauses: so that the headform falls straight down, otherwise the helmet fails.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 10 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: motion of the headform white arrow, signal of the accelerometer cyan dots on a cyan cable, temperature blue (cold) and red (hot), evaluation curve green with a red limit line, helmet purple shell and white foam. Sizes and speeds are not to scale.
  • Simplifications: the impact is shown in slow motion; the headform hangs upside down on the carriage so that the crown hits the anvil; the technician is a simple figure; the wet condition is not shown (the chamber shows cold and hot); the numbers 5.4 meters per second and 250 g appear in the text but not in the clips.
  • Students often confuse the headform (part 3, the metal head) with the helmet (part 1, the product under test), the accelerometer (part 4, the sensor) with the data acquisition system (part 9, records the signal), and the drop rig (part 5, frame with guide wires) with the release mechanism (part 6, holds and releases). The helmet appears in stations 1, 2 and 6.
  • The stations: 1 conditioning, 2 mounting, 3 lifting, 4 impact, 5 recording, 6 evaluation.
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from biomechanics or sports engineering to look at it before the first class.
