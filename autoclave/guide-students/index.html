Describe a process: student guide

You work in pairs on two laptops. One of you watches short videos of a medical process. The other listens and writes. Together you write a complete description of how the process works. The example here is steam sterilization, the way a hospital autoclave makes surgical instruments sterile before an operation.

Before you start

  • Find your partner and decide who starts as the one who watches (the hand) and who listens and writes (the voice).
  • Open the link your lecturer gives you. Each part has its own link.
  • Get the class code from your lecturer. You need it to join the writing area.
  • Use a nickname, not your real name. Do not write personal details.

Part 1: learn the parts

  1. Open the vocabulary sheet. You see a picture of the autoclave with numbers.
  2. Choose the right name for each number from the word box. Every name is used once.
  3. Write in your own words what each part does.
  4. Compare your answers with your partner. Check them with the pictures of the stations on page 2.

Finish Part 1 before you go on. You will use these names in your description.

Part 2: describe what you see

Your roleWhat you do
Hand (watches)Open the link and choose "I watch and describe". Watch each station. Tell your partner what happens: what moves, where it goes, what changes. Use the names from Part 1.
Voice (writes)Do not look at your partner's screen. Open the link and choose "I listen and write". Join the writing area with the class code. Take notes and ask questions.
  • Each station plays only once. You cannot pause or go back, so describe while you watch.
  • The six stations follow the instruments through one sterilization cycle: loading, air removal, steam, holding, steam release, drying and protocol.
  • The voice may ask questions, for example: What happens next? Who does this? What is this part called?

Part 3: write and check

  1. Both of you open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code.
  2. Write one complete description of the process together, from start to finish.
  3. Use the names from Part 1 and connectors such as first, then, after that, finally.
  4. Watch any station again on the right of the screen to check your text. You can replay as often as you like.
  5. Read your description aloud once to check that nothing is missing.

To describe a stepTo connect steps

If something goes wrong

  • The video does not start: reload the page and click Play. Try another browser (Chrome or Safari).
  • You lost your connection or joined late: open the link again, enter the class code and your nickname, and tell your lecturer.
  • The writing area does not appear: use the button "Open in new tab" above it.
  • You do not know a word: ask your partner or your lecturer. Do not look up the answers on the internet.
