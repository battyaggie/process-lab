Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. Scrum is a framework in which a team develops a product in sprints of two weeks: the Product Owner is responsible for the product backlog and presents the most important items at the start of the sprint; the development team selects the items it can finish and moves them into the sprint backlog on the Scrum board (To Do, In Progress, Done); every day the team meets at the board for the daily scrum (15 minutes), where each person says what he or she has done and what is in the way; the Scrum Master helps the team and removes obstacles; the notes move across the board and the burndown chart shows the work left; at the end there is a working increment, which the team shows to the stakeholders in the sprint review, where the Product Owner adds new ideas to the backlog; in the retrospective the team discusses what went well and what to improve and writes the results on the retrospective board; then the next sprint starts.

Before class

  1. Part 1 is an online drag-and-drop exercise: nothing needs to be printed. Students check their own answers on screen.
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs. They drag each name from the word box to the right number: a correct name snaps into place, a wrong one jumps back. The station pictures below the table show the steps in more detail.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is selected, is moved, is presented, is discussed, is shown, is added, is removed. Also relative clauses: the team, which builds the product.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 11 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: To Do notes red, In Progress notes yellow, Done notes green, backlog cards blue, feedback speech bubbles with green ticks, time as a ring calendar.
  • Simplifications: people are simple figures marked by color and hats; the text follows the Scrum Guide; the daily scrum is called a meeting but is shown as a place at the board (a ring on the floor with a clock); the retrospective board shows a smile and a frown.
  • Students often confuse the product backlog (part 2, all ideas) with the sprint backlog (part 4, selected items), the Product Owner (part 1, decides what) with the Scrum Master (part 6, helps the team) and the sprint review (shows the increment to the stakeholders) with the retrospective (part 11, what to improve). Parts 1, 2, 3 and 9 appear in more than one station.
  • The stations: 1 the backlog, 2 sprint planning, 3 daily scrum, 4 working, 5 the increment, 6 sprint review, 7 retrospective.
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from business informatics, computer science or industrial engineering to look at it before the first class.
