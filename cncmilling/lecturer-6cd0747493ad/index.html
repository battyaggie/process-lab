Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. This process is making a part on a CNC milling machine: the engineer designs the part on the CAD/CAM computer, the software calculates the tool paths and the G-code, and the controller runs the program. The operator clamps the workpiece in the vise, the probe finds its position, the tool changer takes the cutters from the tool magazine to the spindle, the machine table moves the workpiece in X and Y while the spindle removes the material, the coolant nozzle sprays coolant, and the chip conveyor carries the chips away. Finally the part is measured on the coordinate measuring machine. The 3D model shows the machine behind a glass safety door in six stations. Part 1 uses 12 numbered parts.

Before class

  1. Print or share the student vocabulary sheet (or let students use it on screen from the Part 1 link).
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs on the sheet. The names are not given in the pictures, so they have to use the word box and the station pictures.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is designed, is calculated, is sent, is clamped, is changed, is removed, is measured.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 12 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: G-code data is cyan dots from the computer to the controller, coolant is light blue dots, chips are silver particles, and the surface of the workpiece turns from bright to dark gray where material is removed. A green mark shows that the part is inside the tolerance.
  • The text uses aluminum and a spindle speed of 10,000 revolutions per minute (the clips only show the rotation). X and Y are the two horizontal directions of the machine table, Z is the vertical movement of the spindle; explain this only if the class needs it.
  • Students often confuse the tool magazine (part 6, stores the tools) with the tool changer (part 7, moves them), the probe (part 5, touches the workpiece in the machine) with the coordinate measuring machine (part 12, measures the finished part), the spindle (part 8) with the cutter, and the controller (part 2) with the CAD/CAM computer (part 1). The spindle appears in stations 3, 4 and 5.
  • The stations: 1 programming (the part is modeled, the tool paths are drawn, the G-code goes to the controller), 2 setting up (the safety door opens, the block is lowered into the vise, the probe touches it at four points, a coordinate cross appears), 3 tool change (the magazine turns, the changer puts the face mill into the spindle), 4 milling (the table moves, coolant sprays, chips fall onto the conveyor), 5 drilling (the changer fetches a drill, four holes), 6 inspection (the coordinate measuring machine measures the part, a green mark appears). The operator is not shown, and the machine has no roof so that the camera can look inside.
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from Maschinenbau to look at it before the first class.
  • Other exercises are on the same site. Each one has its own address.
