Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. This process is a wastewater treatment plant: a bar screen catches large objects and a grit chamber removes sand, a primary clarifier settles fine solids, bacteria in the aeration tank (with air from a blower and diffusers) break down the dissolved dirt, a secondary clarifier settles the activated sludge, a pump returns most of the sludge to the aeration tank while the excess sludge is digested into biogas, and the clean water is disinfected with UV light and released into the river through the outfall.

Before class

  1. Part 1 is an online drag-and-drop exercise: nothing needs to be printed. Students check their own answers on screen.
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs. They drag each name from the word box to the right number: a correct name snaps into place, a wrong one jumps back. The station pictures below the table show the steps in more detail.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is screened, is removed, settles, is pumped, is mixed, is broken down, is released.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 11 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: raw wastewater is brown, clean water is blue, air is white, sludge is dark brown, biogas is yellow and UV light is purple. Everything flows from the left (sewer) to the right (river); the return sludge line runs back to the left below the tanks.
  • Simplifications: the plant is stylised and not to scale. Real plants have several parallel lines, chemical dosing for phosphorus and more control equipment. UV disinfection is not used in every plant; many plants release the water without it. The bacteria and oxygen inset was left out.
  • Students often confuse the primary clarifier (part 3, before aeration, settles raw solids) with the secondary clarifier (part 7, after aeration, settles activated sludge), the aeration tank (part 4, with air) with the sludge digester (part 9, closed, heated, no air, makes biogas), and the blower (part 5, makes the air) with the diffusers (part 6, release it as bubbles). Primary sludge, return sludge and excess sludge are different streams. The aeration tank (4) appears in stations 3 and 5.
  • The stations: 1 screening and grit removal, 2 primary settling, 3 aeration, 4 secondary settling, 5 sludge return and digestion, 6 disinfection and discharge.
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from environmental or process engineering to look at it before the first class.
