Describe a process: lecturer manual

This exercise trains describing a technical process in English. Pairs work on two laptops. One student (the hand) watches short station videos and describes them. The other (the voice) listens, takes notes and writes. There are no instructions to relay: the task is watch, describe, write. This process is a surface-mount line: the board loader puts a bare printed circuit board (PCB) on the conveyor. In the stencil printer a squeegee presses solder paste through a stencil onto the pads. The SPI machine measures the paste with a laser. The pick-and-place machine takes components from the feeders with a vacuum nozzle, checks them with a camera and places them on the paste. The board then goes through the reflow oven: in the preheat zone it heats up slowly, in the peak zone (about 245 degrees Celsius) the paste melts, in the cooling zone the solder solidifies. The AOI machine photographs the board and compares it with a reference. Good boards are stacked in the unloader, boards with defects go to the rework station. The 3D model has seven stations. Part 1 uses 11 numbered parts.

Before class

  1. Print or share the student vocabulary sheet (or let students use it on screen from the Part 1 link).
  2. Open the writing app, start a session and create the writing exercise for the day. The class code appears when you create the exercise.
  3. Decide the pairs. Each pair needs two laptops.
  4. Keep the lecturer vocabulary sheet at hand. It has the answers and sample wording for what each part does.
  5. Test the three links on one laptop, including joining your own session in the writing area.

The links

The site is public, so students do not need an account. Give out one link at a time, when that part starts.

  • Part 1: vocabulary sheet
  • Part 2: watch and describe
  • Part 3: write and check

The Part 2 link asks students to choose a role: "I watch and describe" (the videos) or "I listen and write" (the writing area, no videos).

Running the parts

Part 1: vocabulary

  • Students work alone or in pairs on the sheet. The names are not given in the pictures, so they have to use the word box and the station pictures.
  • Check the table together. Have students say each name aloud. Accept other sensible wording for the functions.

Part 2: blind run

  • Make sure the voice does not see the hand's screen (seat them back to back or at an angle).
  • Each station plays once. The page remembers which stations a student has watched, so a reload does not give a second viewing. This is an honor system, not a lock.
  • Use the button "Reset stations" at the bottom of the hand's page if a student needs to start again.
  • Listen in on a few pairs. Notice whether they use the Part 1 names and connectors.

Part 3: write and check

  • Both students open the Part 3 link and join the writing area with the class code and a nickname.
  • Pairs write one complete description. They replay stations on the right of the screen to check it.
  • You can follow all groups in the teacher view of the writing app.

Part 4: feedback

  • Use the group swap in the writing app so that groups read and comment on each other's work.

Language focus

  • Describing sequence: first, then, after that, finally.
  • Process verbs and passive voice: is loaded, is printed, is measured, is picked, is placed, is melted, is inspected.
  • Technical vocabulary for the 11 parts and their functions.

Good to know

  • Check the three links and typing between two laptops before the first class.
  • Students use nicknames. The writing app runs on Vercel and Firebase, so tell students not to enter personal details.
  • Colors in the clips: the board is green, solder paste silver-gray, heat is orange (preheat) and red-orange (peak), cooling air is blue, inspection data is cyan, a defect is marked red.
  • The oven has three numbered zones (6, 7, 8) but is one machine. In the model the boards and components are about 1.5 times larger than real so that they are visible; lead-free solder peaks at about 245 degrees Celsius.
  • Students often confuse the SPI machine (part 3, checks the paste) with the AOI machine (part 9, checks the finished board), the feeders (part 4, hold the components) with the pick-and-place machine (part 5), and the unloader (part 10) with the rework station (part 11). The pick-and-place machine appears in stations 3 and 4.
  • The stations: 1 loading and printing (the board enters, the stencil lowers, the squeegee spreads paste), 2 paste inspection (a laser line scans, the screen shows a map), 3 component supply (the feeders and the head, the nozzle picks a component), 4 placement (camera check, the components are placed), 5 reflow (preheat, peak, cooling), 6 inspection (flash, comparison, green tick), 7 sorting (a good board is pushed into the unloader, a defective board goes to the rework station where a technician repairs it).
  • The technical content is simplified for B2 learners and has not been checked by a subject lecturer. Ask a colleague from Elektronik to look at it before the first class.
